# Citation conventions

The `citation` field must let a reader find the original without asking you. Pick one
style per jurisdiction and hold to it.

> **Set the house style here before the first entry goes in.** Until it is decided,
> follow the jurisdiction's own convention and note which you used.

## Minimum elements

Whatever the style, a citation here must carry enough to locate the document:

| Source | Must include |
| --- | --- |
| Statute | Code or act name, section, and version/year of the text quoted |
| Regulation | Register or code cite, section, and the date or issue quoted from |
| Case | Party names, reporter or neutral citation, court, year — and pinpoint |
| Treaty | Title, parties or opening-for-signature date, and treaty series cite |
| Guidance | Issuer, document title and number, date of issue |
| Secondary | Author, title, edition or volume, publisher, year, pinpoint |

Add a pinpoint (section, paragraph, page) whenever you rely on a specific passage. A
cite to a whole statute when you mean one subsection is not a cite.

---

## House rules

1. **Cite the version you read.** Law is versioned. `17 U.S.C. § 107 (2018)` and
   `17 U.S.C. § 107 (1976)` are different claims.
2. **Prefer neutral or official citations** where the jurisdiction issues them.
3. **Parallel citations** go in the `## Provenance` section, not in `citation`.
4. **URLs are provenance, not citation.** The formal cite goes in `citation`; the link
   goes in `official_source_url`. Links rot; citations do not.
5. **Short forms** are fine in prose inside `## Notes`, once the full cite has appeared
   in the frontmatter. Never in the `citation` field itself.
6. **Unpublished, withdrawn, or non-precedential?** Say so in the citation parenthetical
   and set `authority_level` accordingly.
