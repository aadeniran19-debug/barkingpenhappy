My recommendation A + B

Use Pattern A for section pages — you scan and expand without losing the list — and have every card also reachable as Pattern B at its own anchor, which is where the homepage "What am I being asked?" links land you.

This costs one extra rendering path but gets both behaviours from one set of card data, and it satisfies the brief's stable-ID rule directly: the ID is the anchor, and the anchor is a usable screen. Pattern C is the one I'd drop — it's the most code, and its advantage disappears on a small screen.

2 · "What I say to the bench"

Your brief calls this the only text you would ever say out loud. It has to be unmistakable at a glance, and it must never be confusable with a prompt in a reasons scaffold. Three treatments, same words:

Treatment 3 — full inversion

SAY

Sample wording, layout demonstration only. This is where the advice formulation sits, in the words you would actually use.

Why treatment 1. It differs from body text on three channels at once — colour, border weight, and an explicit SAY label — so it survives a bad projector, a greyscale printout, and colour-blindness. Treatment 3 reads well on screen but inverts to a solid black block when printed, and prompts in scaffolds need to be visually weaker than surrounding text, which is easier to arrange against 1 than against 3.

3 · Gap markers

You left this to me. Proposal, in three parts:

In the page — a dashed red block that cannot be mistaken for content and cannot sit inside a SAY panel:

⚠ GAP — provision not verified. Nothing to rely on here yet.

In the source — a greppable token, so every outstanding gap can be listed with one search rather than by reading the file:

[[GAP: PD2C para list — not verified]]

On the homepage — a live count ("14 gaps") linking to a list of every one, so the unfinished parts are visible rather than buried.

The rule I'd enforce in the build: a gap marker is never permitted inside a SAY panel. If the wording isn't verified, the panel doesn't render at all — the card shows the gap where the panel would be. That way anything sitting in a SAY box is, by construction, something checked.
